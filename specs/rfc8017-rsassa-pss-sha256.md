--- abstract

A profile of RFC 8017 for nish/crypto/rsa-sign: an RSA private key read
from PKCS #1's RSAPrivateKey, the RSASP1 primitive under it, and
RSASSA-PSS signing with SHA-256 and MGF1 with SHA-256, the TLS 1.3 scheme
rsa\_pss\_rsae\_sha256 of RFC 8446. Every requirement quotes RFC 8017, the
main source, or RFC 8446; every binding sentence of RFC 8017 (MUST, MUST
NOT, SHALL, SHALL NOT, REQUIRED) is tested or excluded here.

# Introduction

{::boilerplate bcp14-tagged}

nish/crypto/rsa-sign reads a two-prime RSAPrivateKey (RFC 8017 Section
A.1.2) of 2048 to 4096 bits from the bytes [start, end) of an array and
holds it as a `Secret`; the properties see that `Secret` as bytes, and
pass it back as they got it. `rsaSignRaw` is RSASP1 (Section 5.2.1) on a
k-octet big-endian message representative, k the modulus's length in
octets. `rsaSignPssSha256Salted` is RSASSA-PSS-SIGN (Section 8.1.1) with
EMSA-PSS-ENCODE (Section 9.1.1) over SHA-256, MGF1 with SHA-256 and the
salt the caller gives; `rsaSignPssSha256` derives a 32-octet salt (below).
Parsing and signing return null on any failure. `rsaVerifyPssDer`, from
nish/crypto/rsa, is RSASSA-PSS-VERIFY under a PKCS #1 RSAPublicKey (Section
A.1.1) with the hash it is told (1 is SHA-256) and the salt length it is
told; it is the independent check every signing property uses.

Every property signs under one fixed RSA-2048 key, written out as its DER
in each property, made by OpenSSL 3.0.13 (`openssl genrsa 2048`), and its
public key, `openssl rsa -RSAPublicKey_out -outform DER`. The two
known answers are OpenSSL's too: P-PSS-KNOWN is `openssl pkeyutl -sign
-rawin -digest sha256 -pkeyopt rsa_padding_mode:pss -pkeyopt
rsa_pss_saltlen:0` over "sample", and P-RAW-KNOWN is `openssl rsautl
-sign -raw` over the 256 octets 00 to ff, also checked against a
square-and-multiply in BigInt.

Out of scope: rsaSignPssSha256Into, the same signature as rsaSignPssSha256 written into a caller's array through an RsaSignScratch, held by tests/link/crypto\_rsa\_sign; its first parameter is the class RsaSignScratch, which a ts-interface block refuses

Out of scope: rsaSignPssSha256SaltedInto, the same signature as rsaSignPssSha256Salted written into a caller's array through an RsaSignScratch, held by tests/link/crypto\_rsa\_sign; its first parameter is the class RsaSignScratch, which a ts-interface block refuses

Out of scope: RsaSignScratch, the reusable scratch of the two Into signers, a class and not a function

Out of scope: RSA\_SIGN\_MIN\_BITS, an `i32` constant a property cannot call; every property reads a 2048-bit key, which is that minimum

Out of scope: RSA\_SIGN\_MAX\_BITS, an `i32` constant a property cannot call; a 4096-bit key is held by tests/link/crypto\_rsa\_sign

Out of scope: RSA\_PSS\_SHA256\_SALT\_SIZE, an `i32` constant a property cannot call; P-SALT-DERIVED holds the 32-octet salt

# Out of Scope

RFC 8017 prints no RSA key, signature or intermediate value, so this
profile has no vectors: every clause is a property over the fixed key the
Introduction names, and the two known answers are OpenSSL's, checked as
properties.

nish/crypto/rsa-sign derives the salt of `rsaSignPssSha256` where Section
9.1.1 step 4 generates a random one: the SHA-256 of the octets of "nish
rsa-pss-sha256 salt", the private exponent d as k big-endian octets, and
mHash. Note 4 of Section 8.1 keeps a security reduction for any salt, the
empty one included, and a verifier cannot tell a derived salt from a drawn
one. P-SALT-DERIVED holds the derivation, and P-SALTED holds that any salt
the caller gives is encoded as step 4 says.

Multi-prime keys (version 1, Section 3.2's triplets r\_i, d\_i, t\_i and
the steps 2.b.2 and 2.b.5 of Section 5.2.1) are refused, not implemented:
P-KEY-VERSION holds the refusal.

Keys below 2048 bits or above 4096 bits are refused, and so is a key whose
p or q is longer than half the modulus rounded up to 32 bits; RFC 8017
sets no size, and the keys OpenSSL, Go and BoringSSL generate have primes
of half the modulus's bits.

RSASSA-PSS-VERIFY (Section 8.1.2), RSAES (Section 7), RSASSA-PKCS1-v1\_5
(Section 8.2) and the ASN.1 of the algorithm identifiers (Appendix A.2) are
nish/crypto/rsa's or not implemented. `rsaVerifyPssDer` is the check here,
not a function under test.

Sections 5.2.1 and 8.1 ask for no defence against timing or faults in
binding words. nish/crypto/rsa-sign computes with the secret values in
constant time, held by tests/cases/ct\_asm\_rsa.ts, and raises every
signature to e and compares it with the message representative before it
returns it, against the fault that factors n from one faulty CRT half
(docs/security/crypto-rsa.md, RSA-S2). Neither is observable by a
functional test.
